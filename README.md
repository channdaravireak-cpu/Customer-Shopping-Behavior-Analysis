# Customer-Shopping-Behavior-Analysis

An end-to-end data analytics project analyzing customer shopping behavior using **Python, SQL, MySQL, and Power BI**.

The project focuses on understanding customer purchasing patterns, product performance, discount usage, subscription behavior, customer loyalty, and demographic trends. The analysis combines data cleaning and feature engineering in Python, business analysis in SQL, and interactive visualization in Power BI.

## Project Overview

This project analyzes a dataset containing **3,900 customer shopping records and 18 columns**.

The main goal is to answer business questions such as:
- What is the total revenue generated by male vs. female customers?
- Which customers used a discount but still spent more than the average purchase amount?
- Which are the top 5 products with the highest average review rating?
- Compare the average Purchase Amounts between Standard and Express Shipping.
- Do subscribed customers spend more? Compare average spend and total revenue between subcribers and non-subscribers.
- Which 5 products have the highest percentage of purchases with discount applied?
- Segment customers into New, Returning, and Loyal based on their total number of previous purchases, and show the count of each statement.
- What are the top 3 most purchased products within each category?
- Are customers who are repeat buyers (more than 5 previous purchases) also likely to subscribe?
- What is the revenue contribution of each age group?

- The project follows an end-to-end analytics workflow:

**Raw Data → Data Cleaning → Feature Engineering → MySQL → SQL Analysis → Power BI Dashboard → Business Insights & Recommendations**

  # Data Cleaning and Preparation

Data preparation was performed using **Python and Pandas** in `notebook.ipynb`.

### 1. Data Inspection

The dataset was initially inspected using:

- `.info()`
- `.describe()`
- `.isnull().sum()`
- Duplicate checks
- Data type inspection
- Basic statistical analysis

This was used to understand the structure, quality, and completeness of the dataset.

### 2. Missing Value Treatment

The dataset contained **37 missing values in `review_rating`**.

Instead of using the overall median rating, missing ratings were filled using the **median rating of the customer's product category**.

This category-aware approach helps preserve differences in rating patterns between categories.

### 3. Column Name Standardization

`pyjanitor` was used to standardize column names into snake_case format.

For example:

```text
Customer ID → customer_id
Purchase Amount → purchase_amount
Review Rating → review_rating
```

The `purchase_amount` column was then renamed to:

```text
purchase_amount_usd
```

This makes the currency unit explicit and improves readability.

### 4. Feature Engineering

Two additional analytical features were created.

#### Age Group

Age was divided into four groups using quartile-based bucketing with `pd.qcut()`:

- Young Adult
- Adult
- Middle-Age
- Senior

This allows customer behavior and revenue to be compared across age segments.

#### Purchase Frequency

The categorical purchase frequency was converted into a numeric day interval.

For example:

```text
Weekly → 7 days
Monthly → 30 days
Quarterly → approximately 90 days
Annually → approximately 365 days
```

This makes purchase frequency easier to analyze quantitatively.

### 5. Redundant Column Removal

`promo_code_used` was removed because it was perfectly correlated with `discount_applied` in this dataset.

Keeping both variables would provide duplicate information without adding analytical value.

### 6. MySQL Loading

After cleaning and transformation, the final DataFrame was loaded into MySQL using SQLAlchemy.

The cleaned data was stored in:

```text
customer_behaviour
```

# Key Findings

### 1. Subscription Does Not Currently Drive Higher Spending

Subscribers have an average purchase amount of **$59.49**, compared with **$59.87** for non-subscribers.

The difference is very small, suggesting that the current subscription program does not provide a meaningful spending uplift.

At the same time, a large number of repeat buyers remain unsubscribed.

### 2. Revenue Is Driven Primarily by Customer Volume

Male customers generate significantly more total revenue, but their average spending is almost identical to female customers.

This indicates that the revenue gap is mainly caused by the larger number of male customers in the dataset.

### 3. Discounts Can Coexist With High Order Values

A total of **839 customers** used discounts while spending above the overall average purchase amount.

This suggests that promotions can encourage purchasing without necessarily causing customers to make low-value orders.

### 4. The Customer Base Is Highly Loyal

Approximately 80% of customers are classified as Loyal.

However, only **83 customers** are classified as New.

This could indicate a mature customer base or a dataset that primarily captures existing customers.

### 5. Age Groups Have Relatively Similar Revenue

Revenue is fairly evenly distributed across the four age groups.

Young Adults generate the highest revenue, but the difference between age groups is not large.

### 6. Express Customers Have Slightly Higher Spending

Express shipping customers have an average purchase amount of **$60.48**, compared with **$58.46** for Standard shipping customers.

This suggests that Express shipping could be positioned as an optional upsell.

# Business Recommendations

## 1. Redesign the Subscription Program

Since subscribers do not currently spend more than non-subscribers, the subscription program should be reviewed.

Potential improvements include:

- Spend-based membership tiers
- Exclusive discounts
- Free or discounted Express shipping
- Early access to products
- Loyalty rewards
- Higher-value benefits for frequent customers

The goal should be to create measurable additional value rather than simply encouraging customers to subscribe.

---

## 2. Target High-Value Discount Users

The 839 customers who used discounts while spending above average represent an interesting customer segment.

Marketing campaigns could target these customers with:

- Personalized product recommendations
- Bundle offers
- Loyalty rewards
- Higher-value purchase incentives
- Limited-time promotions

This could increase customer lifetime value while maintaining promotional engagement.

---

## 3. Focus More on Customer Acquisition

With only **83 New customers** compared with **3,116 Loyal customers**, the dataset has a very strong concentration of existing customers.

The business should consider investing more heavily in:

- New customer acquisition
- Referral campaigns
- Social media campaigns
- First-purchase incentives
- New customer promotions

This can help diversify the customer base and create a stronger future customer pipeline.

---

## 4. Convert Repeat Buyers Into Subscribers

There are thousands of repeat buyers who are not subscribed.

These customers already demonstrate purchasing behavior and may be easier to convert than completely new customers.

Targeted subscription offers could be presented after customers reach certain purchase thresholds.

---

## 5. Promote Express Shipping

Because Express customers have a higher average purchase amount than Standard customers, Express shipping could be positioned as an upsell.

For example:

> "Upgrade to Express Shipping for faster delivery."

This could be particularly effective during checkout for customers with larger baskets.
